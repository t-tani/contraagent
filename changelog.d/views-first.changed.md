The monitor gives its screens to what each one is for. A task's detail opens on its views: its header is the task's name, how it ended when that takes more than a word, and one line of facts (the state, the coding agent, the model, the cost and the time). The rest about the task (locks, tokens, context, usage, the attempts, web searches, changed files) stands beside the views on a wide screen and opens from the line of facts on a narrow one, where the transcript now takes most of the screen. The overview puts a run with no problem and no failed output in one card, with a line for the problems and one for the outputs; a run with problems keeps a card for each. On a phone the header is one line, with one button for the other language, and the clock (the phone shows the time) and the monitor's name left out; a definition opens under its kind, and a second tap closes it. Picking another definition, view or round no longer scrolls the view back to the top.
