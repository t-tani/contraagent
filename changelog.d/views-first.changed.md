The monitor gives its screens to what each one is for. A task's detail opens on its views: its header is the task's name, one line of facts (its state, such as "Done after 1 correction", its cost and its time) and, when there is one, the cause behind the state on a line of its own ("First failed check: ..."), where they were one long line. The rest about the task (who ran it, locks, tokens, context, usage, the attempts, web searches, changed files) stands beside the views on a wide screen and opens from the line of facts on a narrow one, where the transcript now takes most of the screen. The overview puts a run with no problem and no failed output in one card, with a line for the problems and one for the outputs; a run with problems keeps a card for each. On a phone the header is one line, with one button for the other language, and the clock (the phone shows the time) and the monitor's name left out; a definition opens under its kind, and a second tap closes it. Picking another definition, view or round no longer scrolls the view back to the top.
